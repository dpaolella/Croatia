The Dubrovnik opening 🚶 No car needed

Three nights inside (or just outside) the walls, shared by all three routes. The first two days are quieter — solo or a small group — which is a gift: do the things that are nicer without a crowd. By the 21st the whole crew's in and you point the car north.

The Elaphiti are a car-free archipelago

Koločep, Lopud and Šipan have no cars at all — just footpaths, a few electric carts, and boats. Lopud hides Šunj, one of the Adriatic's rare stretches of real sand. An easy, unhurried day out from the Old Port.

🩳
You asked · quick swim, not a beach-club day
Skip the lounger scene at Sveti Jakov

Sveti Jakov is the locals' quieter pick with a knockout view back at the Old Town — but it has a paid-lounger beach bar, about 160 steps down, and it does fill up on sunny afternoons. For a fast dip instead: climb down to the rocks at a Buža cliff bar, drop into little Šulić cove by the Pile gate, or swim off Lokrum. Banje is the central, convenient pebble beach, but loungers are pricey and it gets busy.

Eat like a local

Konobas over white tablecloths

The brief: family-run konobas, market stalls, cheap oysters, and the late-morning marenda — not fine dining. Keep the fancy meal to one, if you do one at all. Here's where to point yourselves in each base.

🍽️
If you do exactly one nice dinner
Make it count — then back to the konobas

For a single splurge, Restaurant 360 on the Dubrovnik ramparts or LD Restaurant on Korčula are the ones worth the white tablecloth. Otherwise: grilled fish, black risotto, peka, and a carafe of the house red. That's the trip.

Car or boat?

How you actually get from Dubrovnik to Split

You spotted it — you don't necessarily need a car. The mountain-and-coast route (A) really does, but you can also island-hop the whole way by catamaran (that's Route C, and a car-free version of B). Here's the trade-off, with rough one-way costs. Treat the numbers as ballpark figures to reconfirm for 2026.

🚗 One-way rental

~€200–350total for the car
  • Compact car, ~3 days (collect leaving Dubrovnik, drop at Split airport): ~€120–210
  • One-way drop fee: ~€0–60 (often small or waived within Croatia)
  • Fuel + tolls — the Pelješac Bridge is free: ~€35–55
  • Parking in towns: ~€10–20/day

Priced per car, split by the group — cheaper per head the more of you there are. It's the only way to reach Biokovo, Omiš, the Makarska Riviera beaches and the Pelješac wine road. Mind the Dubrovnik old-town fine and town parking.

⛴ Foot-passenger catamarans

~€45–90per person, one-way
  • Dubrovnik → Korčula: ~€25–35 pp
  • Korčula → Split: ~€20–30 pp
  • Or one through Dubrovnik → Split fast boat (seasonal, via the islands): ~€40–60 pp

Priced per person — so it scales up with the group. No driving and gorgeous, but schedule-bound (late-September sailings thin out — confirm your dates), foot-passengers only, and you can't reach the mountain or coast stops without local buses or taxis.

🚗 Needs a car

  • Biokovo / Sveti Jure — the big lookout (Route A)
  • Omiš fort & the Cetina canyon (A)
  • Makarska Riviera beaches — Brela, Tučepi (A)
  • Ston oyster stop & the Pelješac wine road
  • Trogir / Šibenik / Krka day trips (B)

🚶 No car needed

  • All of Dubrovnik — Old Town, Srđ (cable car / bus / foot), Lokrum & the Elaphiti by boat
  • Korčula & Lumbarda (C) — catamaran, then local bus, taxi or bike
  • Brač & Hvar (B) — catamaran straight from Split
  • Split itself — palace, Riva, Marjan, markets, all on foot
🧭
Bottom line
Roughly a wash for two or three of you — pick on style, not price

Route A wants a car (the mountain and coast stops are the whole point). Route C is the easy car-free one — catamaran Dubrovnik → Korčula → Split. Route B works either way. The car wins on flexibility and group cost; the boat wins on zero driving and scenery. With four-plus of you the car gets cheaper per head; as a pair, the boat is very competitive.

Getting around

Logistics that apply to all three

The fiddly bits worth locking in early. Distances in miles, temps in °F.

✈︎ Flights & staggered arrivals

Fly into Dubrovnik, home out of Split (open-jaw) to skip backtracking. You land the morning of the 19th; a friend may join the 19th, the last of the crew on the 21st — so the Dubrovnik days flex from solo to full group. Everyone can book the same in-DBV / out-SPU shape without matching exact flights.

🚗 The one-way car

Dubrovnik → Split is about 137–143 mi: the inland A1 motorway plus the Pelješac Bridge runs ~2h50 (no Bosnia border anymore), or the coastal road is ~3h45 and far prettier. Pick the car up on your way out of Dubrovnik and drop it at Split airport.

⛴ Ferries (September)

Split ↔ Hvar and Split ↔ Bol (Brač) catamarans run ~1 hr and frequently, but are foot-passenger only. Dubrovnik ↔ Korčula runs year-round; the Orebić ↔ Korčula car ferry is ~15 min. Book ahead online — late-September sailings thin out.

🏔 Biokovo entry (Route A)

Only 20 cars per hour, tickets online only, about €15 (~$16) per adult; it's 8 mi from the gate up to the Skywalk. Park open roughly April–October. Confirm current hours at pp-biokovo.hr close to your dates.

⚠ Don't drive into Dubrovnik's Old Town

The restricted traffic zone (active Mar–Nov) carries a €260 (~$280) fine for private cars and €1,300+ (~$1,400) for rentals. Park outside the walls and walk in — which is the whole reason you collect the rental on the way out of Dubrovnik, not on arrival.

If you want one answer

Why A fits your plan

It's exactly "three days in Dubrovnik, then drive north": the coast road, one knockout lookout (Biokovo), a fort over the Cetina at Omiš, and a relaxed two-base shape that ends in Split for the flight.

Pick B for water

More swimming and island time — Brač's golden-horn beach and the view above it, with Hvar an easy swap. The least driving, the most boats.

Pick C to slow down

Korčula's little walled town, island whites, and oysters — the gentlest pace and the most eating and drinking. Easy on the legs.

Decide late

Because all three share the Dubrovnik opening, you can choose the road north on the 21st — or even the morning of the 22nd — based on the weather. Save big-lookout days for clear mornings.
