How it's shaped

Land in Dubrovnik, drift north, fly home from Zagreb

You land Saturday morning the 19th; friends trickle in — maybe one on the 19th, the last on Monday the 21st. Every option then funnels to the same ending: everyone sleeps in Split on Thursday the 24th, spends Friday night at Plitvice Lakes, and catches the 6 pm Saturday flight home from Zagreb on the 26th. That's an open-jaw booking — into Dubrovnik, out of Zagreb — with zero backtracking; everyone books that shape, exact flights don't need to match. In between: four ways to spend the week, three opening with Dubrovnik and one (the one worth booking) opening with two nights on a car-free island.

Dubrovnik & the day-trip reach — Mount Srđ above town, Lokrum just offshore, and the car-free Elaphiti islands to the northwest.
⛴️
Cool detail
The Elaphiti are a car-free archipelago

Koločep, Lopud and Šipan have no cars at all — just footpaths, a few electric carts, and boats. Lopud hides Šunj, one of the Adriatic's rare stretches of real sand. An easy, unhurried day out from the Old Port.

🩳
You asked · quick swim, not a beach-club day
Skip the lounger scene at Sveti Jakov

Sveti Jakov is the locals' quieter pick with a knockout view back at the Old Town — but it has a paid-lounger beach bar, about 160 steps down, and it does fill up on sunny afternoons. For a fast dip instead: climb down to the rocks at a Buža cliff bar, drop into little Šulić cove by the Pile gate, or swim off Lokrum. Banje is the central, convenient pebble beach, but loungers are pricey and it gets busy.

Everyone does this · Sept 25–26

The shared finale: Plitvice, then the flight home

The Saturday-evening Zagreb flight is what makes this possible: Plitvice Lakes sits right on the road from Split to Zagreb, so whichever middle you pick, the last two days are the same. Friday: easy Split morning, collect the car (boat crews rent here; drivers already have one), roll north with an optional stop, sleep by the park. Saturday: the lakes on foot all morning, then Zagreb and the 6 pm flight to Berlin.

Split → Plitvice ~155 mi (~2.5–3 hrs) · Plitvice → Zagreb Airport ~85 mi (~2 hrs). Optional breaks: Trogir or Krka on the way up, Rastoke on the way out.
🎟️
Book this when you book flights
Plitvice is timed-entry — and September 26 is still peak pricing

Adult day tickets are ~€40 (~$43) through Sept 30 (they drop from October), entry is a timed one-hour window, the daily cap is ~8,000 people, and morning slots sell out weeks ahead — buy on the official site (np-plitvicka-jezera.hr). The ticket includes the boat across Lake Kozjak and the shuttle train. Do the Lower Lakes to Veliki Slap (Croatia's tallest waterfall, ~256 ft), cross the lake, and walk as much of the Upper Lakes as the clock allows — 4–5 hrs is a solid visit. No swimming here — it's a UNESCO park. One more thing: the park sits at ~2,000 ft, so mornings run 50s°F — pack a layer.

If you do exactly one nice dinner
Make it count — then back to the konobas

For a single splurge, Restaurant 360 on the Dubrovnik ramparts or LD Restaurant on Korčula are the ones worth the white tablecloth. Otherwise: grilled fish, black risotto, peka, and a carafe of the house red. That's the trip.

Car or boat?

How you actually get from Dubrovnik to Split

You spotted it — you don't need a car for most of this. The mountain-and-coast route (A) really does, but routes C and D float all the way to Split by catamaran, and B works either way. The one constant: every route rents a car in Split for the Plitvice finale (Sept 25–26) and drops it at Zagreb airport. Here's the trade-off, with rough costs — ballpark figures to reconfirm for 2026.

🚗 The car way (Route A, or B by car)

~€260–470total for the car · 5 days, DBV → ZAG
  • Compact car, ~5 days (collect leaving Dubrovnik on the 22nd, drop at Zagreb airport the 26th): ~€160–300
  • One-way drop fee within Croatia: ~€0–80
  • Fuel + tolls for the whole run — the Pelješac Bridge is free: ~€90–110
  • Parking in towns: ~€10–20/day

Priced per car, split by the group — cheaper per head the more of you there are. It's the only way to reach Biokovo, Omiš, the Makarska Riviera beaches and the Pelješac wine road. Mind the Dubrovnik old-town fine and town parking.

⛴ The boat way (Routes C & D, or B by ferry)

~€50–100per person in ferries · + the shared finale car
  • Dubrovnik → Mljet (Route D): ~€10–25 pp · Mljet → Dubrovnik the same
  • Dubrovnik → Korčula: ~€25–35 pp · Korčula → Split: ~€20–30 pp
  • Then the finale rental everyone needs — Split → Zagreb, 2 days: ~€80–150 + ~€55 fuel/tolls, per car, split

Ferries are priced per person — so they scale up with the group. No driving and gorgeous (the Korčula→Split run is basically a sightseeing cruise), but schedule-bound: late-September sailings thin out, so confirm your exact dates and book ahead.

🚗 Needs a car

  • Plitvice & the run to Zagreb — Sept 25–26, every route (buses exist but eat the day)
  • Biokovo / Sveti Jure — the big lookout (Route A)
  • Omiš fort & the Cetina canyon (A)
  • Makarska Riviera beaches — Brela, Tučepi (A)
  • Ston oysters & the Pelješac wine road · Trogir / Šibenik / Krka (car versions of B/C)

🚶 No car needed

  • All of Dubrovnik — Old Town, Srđ (cable car / bus / foot), Lokrum & the Elaphiti by boat
  • Mljet (D) — catamaran from Dubrovnik, then bikes and the park boat inside
  • Korčula & Lumbarda (C, D) — catamaran, then local bus, taxi or bike
  • Brač & Hvar (B) — catamaran straight from Split
  • Split itself — palace, Riva, Marjan, markets, all on foot
🧭
Bottom line
Roughly a wash for two or three of you — pick on style, not price

Route A wants the car the whole way (the mountain and coast stops are the point). Routes C and D float to Split — no driving until the finale. Route B works either way. And since everyone converges on Split by Thursday night, the group can even split up — a car crew on A, a boat crew on D — and reunite for the drive to the lakes. The car wins on flexibility and per-head cost for a bigger group; the boats win on zero driving and scenery.

Getting around

Logistics that apply to all three

The fiddly bits worth locking in early. Distances in miles, temps in °F.

✈︎ Flights & staggered arrivals

Fly into Dubrovnik, home out of Zagreb (open-jaw) — the anchor is the 6 pm ZAG → Berlin departure on Saturday the 26th. You land Saturday morning the 19th; a friend may join that day, the last of the crew lands Monday the 21st. Everyone books the same in-DBV / out-ZAG shape without matching exact flights.

🚗 The one-way car

Dubrovnik → Split is about 137–143 mi (A1 + the Pelješac Bridge ~2h50, or the gorgeous coastal road ~3h45). Then Split → Plitvice ~155 mi (~2.5–3 hrs) and Plitvice → Zagreb Airport ~85 mi (~2 hrs). One-way drops (DBV → ZAG, or SPU → ZAG for the boat crews) are routine within Croatia.

⛴ Ferries (September)

Dubrovnik ↔ Mljet (Pomena/Polače) catamarans run daily in September, ~1.5 hrs (TP-Line, Krilo). Dubrovnik → Korčula ~2 hrs; Korčula → Split ~2.5–3 hrs, some via Hvar/Brač. Split ↔ Bol and Split ↔ Hvar run ~1 hr. All foot-passenger only. Book ahead online — late-September sailings thin out.

🏞 Plitvice entry (everyone, Sept 26)

Timed-entry tickets, ~€40 (~$43) adult through Sept 30, daily cap ~8,000, and morning slots sell out weeks ahead — book on np-plitvicka-jezera.hr as soon as flights are set. Ticket includes the Kozjak boat and shuttle train. No swimming; pack a layer for ~2,000 ft mornings.

🏔 Biokovo entry (Route A)

Only 20 cars per hour, tickets online only, about €15 (~$16) per adult; it's 8 mi from the gate up to the Skywalk. Park open roughly April–October. Confirm current hours at pp-biokovo.hr close to your dates.

🛶 Mljet entry (Route D)

The national park covers the island's lake-filled western end — entry roughly €20 (~$22) in high season (2026 list is posted on np-mljet.hr), and it includes the boat to St Mary's islet. Bikes rent by the hour in Pomena and Polače; the Veliko jezero loop is an easy ride.

⚠ Don't drive into Dubrovnik's Old Town

The restricted traffic zone (active Mar–Nov) carries a €260 (~$280) fine for private cars and €1,300+ (~$1,400) for rentals. Park outside the walls and walk in — which is the whole reason you collect the rental on the way out of Dubrovnik, not on arrival.

Why it wins

Mljet fills the solo window (Sat–Sun) with the trip's calmest corner — saltwater lakes, a monastery islet, bikes instead of cars — and the ~11:10 boat has you back on Dubrovnik's dock at lunchtime Monday, right as the last arrival lands. Then the ferries sightsee for you all the way to Split, and Plitvice caps the week with zero backtracking.

The catch: book it early

D commits you before you fly — the arrival-day catamaran to Mljet, two island nights, the Plitvice timed slot and the 6 pm ZAG seat. Routes A–C share the Dubrovnik opening, so they stay swappable until the morning of the 22nd if you'd rather decide off the weather.

If not D

A is the driver's trip — the coast road and Biokovo's big lookout. B is maximum swimming (Brač's golden horn). C is the gentlest — Korčula, wine, and long lunches.

Everyone meets in Split anyway

All four routes sleep in Split on Thursday the 24th and at Plitvice on Friday — so the group can even run two middles in parallel (a car crew, a boat crew) and reunite for the finale without touching anyone's flights.
